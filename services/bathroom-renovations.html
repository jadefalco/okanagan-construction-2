Bathroom Renovations

Bathroom Renovations in Kelowna

A bathroom is the smallest room we build and the least forgiving. Everything is tight, everything is wet, and every detail is seen from two feet away.

Bathroom renovations across the Okanagan, built to stay dry

Most bathroom problems we're called in to fix are water problems. A shower pan that was never properly sloped. Tile set straight onto drywall. A fan that vents into an attic instead of outside. None of it shows on day one — all of it shows in year five. So the parts of a bathroom renovation nobody photographs are the parts we're most careful about.

That means proper waterproofing membranes behind tile and under shower floors, blocking put in before the drywall goes on so grab bars and glass panels have something solid to land in, ventilation ducted fully to the exterior, and plumbing laid out so future service doesn't mean cutting open finished walls. Then the visible work — tile, vanity, lighting, glass, trim — goes on top of a base that will hold.

We handle main bathrooms, ensuites, powder rooms, and basement bathrooms where drainage has to be worked out below slab. Small footprint or large, the sequence is the same: demolition, framing corrections, plumbing and electrical rough-in, inspection, waterproofing, tile, then finishing.

What a bathroom renovation with us typically includes

  • — Layout planning, including moving fixtures where the plumbing allows
  • — Framing corrections and solid blocking for glass, benches, and grab bars
  • — Waterproofing membranes, sloped shower pans, and curbless entries
  • — Plumbing and electrical rough-in, new circuits, and GFCI protection
  • — Tile setting for floors, walls, and full-height showers
  • — Heated floor systems, vanities, glass, and lighting
  • — Ventilation ducted properly to the exterior

Ensuites, guest baths, and everything smaller

Bathroom renovations in Kelowna often come as part of something larger — a primary ensuite reworked while a bedroom is being reconfigured, or a guest bath brought up to standard before family arrives for the summer. We're just as happy taking on a single powder room. What we won't do is start swinging hammers before we know what's behind the wall, because in a bathroom that's where the cost hides.

We serve Kelowna, West Kelowna, Lake Country, Peachland, Summerland, and Penticton. Many of the homes we work on were built well before current moisture and ventilation standards, and bringing them up to date is a routine part of the job rather than a surprise.

Budget, tile, and where the money actually goes

The honest answer on budget is that bathrooms cost more per square foot than any other room in a house, and the reason is density. A forty-square-foot room can contain plumbing for three fixtures, full waterproofing, tile on five surfaces, electrical, ventilation, glass, and cabinetry. There is very little inexpensive area to average the expensive area against, which is why bathroom quotes surprise people more than kitchen quotes do.

Where it's worth spending: waterproofing, the shower valve, and tile labour — all three are buried and all three are expensive to revisit. Where you can pull back without regret: the vanity, the mirror, and the light fixtures, any of which can be swapped later in an afternoon. Large-format tile costs more per square foot but takes less labour and leaves far fewer grout lines to keep clean, so it often lands close to even. We'll walk through those trade-offs with real numbers before you commit, not after.
